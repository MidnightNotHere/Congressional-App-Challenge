# Demo Video Script (target: ~3 minutes)

Congressional App Challenge submissions are typically capped around 3 minutes —
trim scenes rather than talking faster if it runs long. Record the web app in
a browser at a clean desktop resolution (1280×800 or similar) and, if
possible, cut in a few seconds of the mobile app on a real phone or in Expo
Go — judges respond well to seeing it run on real hardware, not just a
simulator crop.

Screen directions are in **bold**; spoken narration is in plain text. Adjust
timings once you do a read-through out loud — this is paced for a
measured, confident delivery, not a rush.

---

## 0:00–0:15 — Cold open

**Visual:** Start on the Story section hero (NIST Boulder / JILA imagery,
the "$800M+" / "140+" / "#1" stat row).

> "Right now, in Boulder, Colorado, federal researchers are finalizing the
> encryption standards that will protect every bank account, medical
> record, and government system in the country from quantum computers.
> Most Coloradans have never heard of it. This is Quantum4Colorado."

## 0:15–0:45 — The problem

**Visual:** Scroll through the "Why It Changes Everything" explainer and
the ecosystem map tabs (Federal / University / Industry / Policy).

> "Colorado already has the infrastructure other states are spending
> hundreds of millions of dollars to build — NIST Boulder, JILA, CU
> Boulder's quantum program, Quantinuum's headquarters. But there's no
> coordinated state strategy to use it, and no easy way for a resident,
> a business, or a legislator's office to understand what any of this
> means for them. We built Quantum4Colorado to close that gap."

## 0:45–1:45 — Demo: PQC Readiness Tool

**Visual:** Switch to "Is Your Org Ready?" Click through 2–3 questions
quickly (speed up in editing if needed), then land on a completed result:
score ring, risk factors, prioritized action list.

> "The centerpiece is the PQC Readiness Tool. In under three minutes, any
> organization — a small business, a school district, a hospital, a
> state agency — answers questions about the data they handle and the
> systems they run, and gets back a real risk score, the specific
> factors driving it, and a prioritized action list built from actual
> NIST, CISA, and NSA guidance."

**Visual:** Click "Download my organization's report (PDF)", then "Show
QR code" — hold on the QR code for a beat.

> "Results export as a PDF for internal use, or share as a link and QR
> code — so an IT director can hand this straight to their team without
> anyone re-answering the questions."

## 1:45–2:15 — Demo: For Representatives

**Visual:** Scroll the investment-gap chart (Illinois / New York /
California vs. Colorado), then the policy recommendation cards.

> "For Representatives, the app translates the same data into
> legislative terms: how Colorado's investment compares to states already
> acting, and four concrete policy recommendations — each one grounded
> in a real precedent from another state or federal agency."

## 2:15–2:40 — Demo: Youth & Education + bilingual + mobile

**Visual:** Quick cut to the Youth section's career quiz result, then
toggle the language switch to Spanish and show a section re-render, then
(if available) a few seconds of the mobile app running on a phone.

> "For students, a quantum-careers quiz connects their interests to real
> Colorado career paths. And because this is a civic tool, it has to
> reach everyone — the entire app is fully bilingual in English and
> Spanish, and works as both a web app and a native mobile app."

## 2:40–3:00 — Close

**Visual:** Return to the Story hero or a wide shot of the nav showing
all four sections.

> "Quantum4Colorado — built by Colorado students, for Colorado's 6th
> District, to make sure the state that's leading the quantum revolution
> doesn't miss the chance to act on it."

## Shot checklist before recording

- [ ] Clear browser cache / restart dev server so there's no leftover
      console noise or slow first-load stutter on camera.
- [ ] Pre-fill the PQC assessment mentally so you don't fumble answers
      on camera — pick answers that land on a mid-tier result (not the
      lowest, which undersells the tool; not the most alarming, which
      can read as fear-mongering).
- [ ] Turn off OS notifications before recording.
- [ ] Record at 1920×1080 if your recording tool supports it, even if
      the browser window itself is smaller — upscaling looks worse than
      recording at native resolution and cropping.
- [ ] If recording the mobile app, use a device or Expo Go, not just the
      `expo start --web` browser view (you already have that covered by
      the web-app footage) — the goal is proof it runs as a real app.
